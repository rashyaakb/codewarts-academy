The frog jumps with the same upward speed and the same gravity every time. Change only its horizontal speed. Predict where it will land, then test it.

run_speed = 4
x += run_speed
vy += 0.7
y += vy

Python-style excerpt from the reel. This browser activity runs the equivalent rules in JavaScript, at 60 physics steps per second.

Why does this number change the landing?

Horizontal speed controls how far the frog moves sideways during its jump. The jump starts with a vertical speed of −14. Gravity adds 0.7 each step. Since those two values stay fixed, the time available to cross the gap stays the same. At speed 4 the frog falls short; at speed 7 it reaches the far platform.

Try explaining the result together

Before pressing Test, point to where you think the frog will land. After both tests, ask: “What changed? What stayed the same? Why did the frog go farther?” You can draw the two paths on paper too.
